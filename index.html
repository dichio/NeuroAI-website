About the symposium

This symposium brings together neuroscientists and AI researchers around shared scientific questions. The goal is to foster a genuine, ongoing dialogue between the two communities, identifying problems where the fields can meaningfully inform each other. Each edition will focus on a different theme; this inaugural event takes efficiency as its organising principle, exploring what the brain’s remarkable ability to do more with less can teach us about building smarter, leaner AI systems.

Speakers

Session 1Learning efficiency

  • Alexander Mathis EPFL, Lausanne

    A computational neuroscientist, working on reverse-engineering the brain's algorithms to understand it and build better AI. His interests span a huge range, including task-driven models of proprioception, reinforcement learning for musculoskeletal control and machine learning for behavior.

    Website ↗
  • Georgia Koppe Heidelberg University, Heidelberg

    A computational neuroscientist working in NeuroAI, she builds machine-learning models of how brain activity and behaviour evolve over time, and draws on how brains adapt to changing environments to make AI systems learn continually.

    Website ↗
  • Étienne Chassaing Hugging Face, Paris

    A robotics engineer, he studies how to learn robust controllers for physical systems with formal guarantees, at the crossroads of control and AI. He currently works on humanoid robots within Hugging Face’s LeRobot project.

    Website ↗

Session 2Representational efficiency

  • Juan Gallego Champalimaud Foundation, Lisbon

    A systems neuroscientist, he studies how the brain learns and controls movement, combining experiments in humans and rodents with neural recordings, targeted manipulations and computational models.

    Website ↗
  • Alex Cayco-Gajic ENS-PSL, Paris

    An applied mathematician, she combines dynamical systems and statistical modelling to study how learning reshapes neural representations, both in analytically tractable neural network models and high-dimensional neural recordings.

    Website ↗
  • Stéphane d'Ascoli Meta AI, Paris

    An AI scientist, he works at the crossroads of deep learning and neuroscience. His research spans models that predict and decode brain activity, and comparisons of how brains and AI process language. He is also a prolific science communicator.

    Website ↗

Session 3Energy efficiency

  • Pierre-Yves Plaçais ESPCI, Paris

    A physicist turned neurobiologist, he studies how brain energy metabolism shapes memory and behaviour in Drosophila. With his team, he showed that the energy neurons produce is a key factor in whether memories persist.

    Website ↗
  • Menachem Stern AMOLF, Amsterdam

    A theoretical physicist, he studies learning as a physical phenomenon, showing how mechanical and electronic networks can train themselves through local rules, offering a potentially far more energy-efficient alternative to conventional machine learning.

    Website ↗
  • Julie Grollier Université Paris-Saclay/Thales, Paris

    A physicist, she designs brain-inspired chips, combining nanoscale spintronic devices that mimic neurons and synapses with learning algorithms suited to physical hardware. She is a pioneer of energy-efficient neuromorphic computing.

    Website ↗

Session 4Keynote & roundtable

  • Romain Brette Sorbonne Université, Paris

    A theoretical neuroscientist, he studies the roots of cognition in single-celled organisms. In his recent book The Brain, In Theory, he challenges the metaphors at the heart of modern neuroscience, such as codes, information and computation.

    Website ↗
